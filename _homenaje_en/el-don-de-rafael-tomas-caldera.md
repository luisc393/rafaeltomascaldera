## An exceptional person

I have never met Professor Caldera in person, but when in 1988 I read his article “El don de sí” (The Gift of Self) in the journal *Scripta Theologica*, what he wrote there captivated me. His words resonated in my head and in my heart as if we were kindred spirits. Caldera said in beautiful words what I had always thought and had barely managed to express. “The solitude of the wise man is a solitude accompanied,” Caldera stated (p. 678), and since then we have always kept each other company throughout these almost forty years.

It is worth copying a luminous paragraph from that article which helps one grasp the depth of the gift of self. Rafael Tomás writes: “There is, however, one aspect […] that we must examine to complete the analysis of what belongs to the very essence of the gift of self, namely, telling the truth. Expressed in this way, it seems a reality different from love, because their formal characters are different, and so are the human faculties to which they immediately refer, the intellect and the will. They are, instead, moments of one and the same reality, the reality of the gift: there is no love without communication in truth, nor full truth-telling without living in love. In short, man gives himself through truth and love, and reaches his fullness in truth and love” (pp. 675-6).

For Rafael Tomás Caldera, ultimately, love and truth are one, and this is not just theory: he exemplifies it magnificently in his life. That is why he is an exceptional person.

## The office of the wise man

Last June, on hearing the news of the terrible earthquake, I wrote to ask how he was. He answered by email on the 26th, telling me in some detail about the history of earthquakes in Venezuela and the damage known at that point. This is how he described his own experience: “I was alone in Araya when the earthquake struck. On the fifth floor. The building swayed a lot; the structure held. Even so, the plaster in almost the whole flat is cracked, furniture fell over, glass cabinets were smashed. Nothing, in the end, since nothing happened to anyone in the household […], thank God. Rebuilding will be laborious, all the more so in the situation we are in. Anyway. A big hug.”

His life was spared for us. I replied that I was struck that he had been alone on a fifth floor, and I asked him: “Did you think the building might collapse with you in it?” He simply did not answer my question, perhaps to avoid any display.

To anyone who wishes to approach the thought and the person of Rafael Tomás Caldera I would recommend reading his exquisite book *El oficio del sabio* (The Office of the Wise Man), for in the best tradition of Aristotle, Thomas Aquinas and a few others, it shows him to us “as if invested with a mission” (p. 13). What is more, the grave office of the wise man “demands of the subject an enormous dedication: he must dispose his inner life so that the truth may manifest itself in it; he must generously serve his time” (p. 14). What Caldera writes of Thomas applies admirably to his own personal life. He has lived as a wise man, giving himself wholly to the truth and caring for others.

Above all, I want to highlight one key to Rafael Tomás’s thought and life, also in harmony with Aquinas: although wise, or precisely because he is, he has never felt himself the owner of the truth; rather, it is the truth that has always shown itself in his life and in his writing.

## A generous reader

Something truly exceptional in Rafael Tomás is his readiness to read other people’s writings and correct them patiently, if the author asks him to. Not only has he corrected many of my books promptly and wisely; for the last ten years, for example, he has read every month the draft of the monthly post for my blog *Filosofía para el siglo XXI* (Philosophy for the 21st Century) and sent me his comments, always kind and intelligent. As my teacher Alejandro Llano used to say in jest, a new commandment should be added for academics: “Read one another.” Rafael Tomás has kept it wonderfully well.

## Writing for real

Rafael Tomás is a master of the art of writing and of teaching others to write. I copy from his lovely little book *De la lectura y del arte de escribir* (On Reading and the Art of Writing): “What is essential, however and without a doubt, is to write. Every day. Then to watch the masters, how they have done it, how they do it. To find, too, someone who can help us see, and correct, the mistakes made in the first steps. […] Write, erase; write, erase… Give free rein to emotion and thought; amend the expression to refine it and make it more exact, more eloquent.”

I gladly join this public celebration of the teaching of my professor and friend Rafael Tomás Caldera, whom may God keep with us for many years. His life has been a gift to Venezuela, to the university and to many like me.
