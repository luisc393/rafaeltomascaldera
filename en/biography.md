Rafael Tomás Caldera Pietri was born in Caracas on 19 December 1945. He is the son of Rafael Caldera, twice President of Venezuela, and Alicia Pietri.

## Education

He graduated in law from the Universidad Central de Venezuela in 1967. He went on to earn a Master of Arts at the University of Notre Dame (United States) in 1970 and a doctorate at the University of Fribourg (Switzerland) in 1974, with a thesis on judgment by inclination in Saint Thomas Aquinas, published in Paris by Vrin.

## *Vértice* magazine

From 1962 to 1968 he was one of the principal editors of [*Vértice*](https://venezuelaentrelineas.com/vertice/), a university magazine of ideas, arts and letters run by a group of students in Caracas. He sat on its editorial board from the first issue, in May 1962, while still in his final year of secondary school at Colegio San Ignacio, and became its editor-in-chief, a role he kept while studying law at Universidad Central de Venezuela. In that first issue he wrote the article ["La ciudad de las influencias"](https://venezuelaentrelineas.com/vertice/01/la-ciudad-de-las-influencias/) ("The City of Influences").

The issues of *Vértice* are being digitized and can be read (in Spanish) in the [Venezuela entre líneas archive](https://venezuelaentrelineas.com/vertice/).

## Teaching

He is a full professor in the Department of Philosophy at Universidad Simón Bolívar in Caracas, where he teaches Medieval Philosophy and Philosophical Anthropology. He also teaches at Universidad Monteávila.

At Universidad Simón Bolívar he served as head of the Department of Philosophy (1988–1989 and 1992–1994), director of the Division of Social Sciences and Humanities (1989–1991) and member of the Appeals Council (1986–1989). From 1982 to 1983 he directed the Humanities Centre of the Instituto Internacional de Estudios Avanzados (IDEA).

## Academies

- Member of the Pontifical Academy of Saint Thomas Aquinas since 16 December 1999.
- Full member of the Venezuelan Academy of Language (Academia Venezolana de la Lengua), seat Q. He was inducted on 24 January 2011 with the address *El orador en la República* ("The Orator in the Republic").
- Member of the Venezuelan Philosophical Society.

## His work

His thought is rooted in the tradition of Saint Thomas Aquinas and in the realism of authors such as Jacques Maritain and Étienne Gilson. From that ground he has written on knowledge (*The First Intellectual Apprehension*, *To Understand Is to Say*), on love and the person (*On the Nature of Love*, *Open Existence*), on education (*Educating in Venezuela*) and on Venezuelan and Latin American culture (*New World and Colonial Mentality*, *In Search of Our Own Expression*). His books on reading and writing have become a guide for university students.

His books have been published in Caracas, Madrid, Pamplona, Paris and Budapest, and in English by Scepter Publishers.
